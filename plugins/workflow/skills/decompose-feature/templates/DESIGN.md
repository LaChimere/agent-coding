# <Specific design title>

> State the requested design scope, decisions already accepted and explicit revisions. Identify unresolved choices honestly.

Replace the guidance below with connected prose that stands on its own. Merge or omit sections
as appropriate and scale the depth to the consequential choices. Use examples, diagrams and
interface sketches where they help a reader understand or assess the proposal.

## Background and problem

Explain the current behavior, the concrete problem, who is affected and why change is needed.
Cite supporting evidence and give enough context for a reader who has not seen the conversation.

## Goals and non-goals

Describe intended outcomes, observable success criteria and scope explicitly excluded. A short
scenario can make the behavior change concrete. Use measurable targets only when evidence supports them.

## Constraints

Explain compatibility commitments, data safety requirements and resource limits. Distinguish
requirements the proposal must satisfy from choices still to be made.

## Proposed design

Introduce the approach through a concrete example from input or trigger to observable outcome.
Use it to connect responsibility boundaries and important interfaces. Where relevant, explain
state ownership, concurrency, transactions, error propagation, recovery and migration direction.
Include validation or rollout detail when it helps assess the engineering design.

## Decisions and trade-offs

Explain consequential choices, credible alternatives and accepted drawbacks. Compare keeping
current behavior when useful, without requiring a quota of alternatives. Keep trade-offs beside
the relevant design detail when that reads better, and avoid repeating them here.

## Risks and open questions

Identify assumptions that need validation and their impact. Explain which decisions remain
open and which assumptions bound the proposal. Include mitigations or the evidence needed
to decide when known.
